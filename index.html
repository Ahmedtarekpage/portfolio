02 My story
Hey, I'm Ahmed Tarek
"Tracks, not courses." What I tell every student

I learned that the hard way. RoboCup champion at 16, then 3,000+ students taught across four countries — and everywhere the same sight: motivated people drowning in tutorials. Not for lack of effort. For lack of a path.

"Learning is a product problem." What I tell every company

So I built the product instead. Python engineer first — top 3% on Upwork — then Dubai as a product manager, leading AI products end to end: discovery, roadmap, delivery, launch. That work includes government projects in Saudi Arabia and Egypt — a confidential Saudi platform built around Matterport 3D capture, and a national AI and Python programme with Udacity — plus EdTech platforms reaching 10,000+ users across six countries.

Now I do that job on my own product: ashrhly.com, concept to launch, an AI tutor that gives every student one guided track. Engineering taught me how to build. Teaching taught me what to build. Product is where they meet.

04 The track method

Watching videos won't change your life. A track will.

A course gives you lessons. A track gives you an order, a deadline, and proof you can point at. Six steps, and it starts with a conversation.

01
Book a free consultation
Thirty minutes, free. You describe where your learners or your users get stuck, and I tell you honestly whether I am the right person for it.
Book a free consultation
02
Start from the goal
First I need to know your real goal — a job, freelance, or your own business — and your situation: your time, your money, what you can actually commit to. Without a realistic goal you get lost. No single course fits everyone, so the plan is built around you. And we plan backwards, from the end to the start: if the goal is a million dollars a year, a job will not get you there — so the goal has to match the road. That is where a track starts.
03
Cut it into steps
Once the goal is real, we read what it actually requires. If it is a role at a specific company, that role needs ten skills — so those ten become the track, in order, each step small enough to finish this week. We keep breaking it down until you know exactly what to do today.
04
Prove each step
Every step closes with real data — a project, an exam, something you can show — not motivational words. That is how we know where you stand and what comes next.
05
See where you are
Progress you can actually see, so you always know what is next and how far is left.
06
Build and ship it
A written scope, weekly visible progress, decisions written down, and something real that reaches users.
